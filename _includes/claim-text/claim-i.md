# CLAIM I — PUBLIC-DOMAIN MOTION PICTURE ON A MIXED PROTECTED DISC

## Item B. Description of Proposed New Exemption

### 1. Types of copyrighted works

Copyrighted motion-picture compilations or collective works, including
television programs, videos, and supplementary motion-picture works, in which
the same technological measure controls access both to a discrete U.S.
public-domain motion picture and to protected disc material.

### 2. Physical media and devices

The works are stored on lawfully made and lawfully acquired DVDs protected by
CSS, standard Blu-ray Discs protected by AACS and any applicable BD+, or Ultra
HD Blu-ray Discs protected by AACS2. Circumvention would extend only as far as
technically necessary to isolate the public-domain motion picture; no disc
image or protected material may remain.

### 3. Purpose of access

The purpose is to isolate and preserve only audiovisual expression established
through a title- and version-specific analysis to be in the U.S. public domain.
The clean output must exclude all protected expression and compilation
authorship, including protected films, menus, supplements, audio or subtitle
tracks, logos, colorizations, reconstructions, and restoration elements. Once a
forensic audit establishes that the output contains no protected expression,
ordinary copyright law governs its downstream use; no numerical or downstream-use
limit is requested.

### 4. Users seeking access

The users are individuals who, at the time of circumvention, lawfully own,
rent, borrow, or are otherwise authorized to possess, access, and use the
source disc. The clean output may be retained after source access ends. A rented
or borrowed disc must be timely returned; the exemption would not excuse an
independent violation of contract or other law. Continuous ownership and lawful
possession are a severable fallback; ordinary copyright law would continue to
govern downstream uses of the forensically verified clean output.

### 5. Existing and likely barriers

The exemption would apply only when, after reasonable effort, no materially
equivalent unencrypted source of the same version and public-domain content is
reasonably available at a fair price. On a qualifying readable disc, the access
control must be bypassed before the public-domain feature can be isolated, and
a working extraction method makes isolation possible. But for § 1201(a)(1),
the qualifying user could make the noninfringing clean output; the legal
prohibition is the remaining barrier.

The problem is newly recurring: annual maximum-term expiration resumed in 2019
after a twenty-year pause, and new cohorts will enter the public domain during
the exemption period while mixed modern discs remain access-controlled. The
request covers only the user's act under § 1201(a)(1), not trafficking. The
three format branches are independently severable within this one petition.
