# CLAIM D — PREVENTIVE AND LAST-READER PRESERVATION

## 1. Types of copyrighted works

Motion pictures, including television programs, videos, and supplementary
motion-picture works, lawfully made and embodied on the media identified below.

## 2. Physical media, devices, and services

The media are CSS-protected DVD, AACS-protected HD DVD, AACS-protected standard
Blu-ray Disc employing any applicable BD+, and AACS2-protected Ultra HD Blu-ray
Disc. The use requires a compatible personal-computer data reader capable of
recovering the audiovisual data, not a standalone display player. The preventive
primary covers DVD, standard Blu-ray, and Ultra HD Blu-ray before carrier or
reader failure or when a required licensed authentication or playback dependency
is unavailable. HD DVD, standard Blu-ray, and Ultra HD Blu-ray are independently
severable branches of the last-reader alternative.

## 3. Purposes for which access is needed

The owner would circumvent solely to make and retain one secured,
owner-controlled, usable, noncommercial preservation copy while recovery remains
possible. The copy may be used immediately for integrity testing and private
viewing, but may not add a concurrent separate audience, be distributed
separately, publicly performed, used commercially, or made available outside the
permitted household. The original must be retained while in the owner's possession
or control. Upon voluntary separation, the copy must accompany the original
without retention or be destroyed; involuntary loss, destruction, deterioration,
or unreadability does not require destruction of the copy.

## 4. Types of users

The users are individual owners in continuous lawful possession of the particular
physical disc when circumvention occurs. Rented, borrowed, and unlawfully obtained
discs are excluded. Household viewing is included; owner-only use is a severable
fallback.

## 5. Current or likely barriers

CSS, AACS and any applicable BD+, and AACS2 prevent creation of the preservation
copy during the finite period when the disc and compatible reader can still
recover the encrypted work. Circumvention would enable the copy, and the owner
refrains because section 1201(a)(1) prohibits that act. The prohibition is
therefore the but-for legal barrier during the last-safe-read window; after
carrier or reader failure, relief may be physically useless.

The request is newly relevant because evidence developed since the Office's 2021
review includes a defined DVD defect cohort, unavailable identical replacements
for some affected DVD titles, the shutdown of a required licensed UHD-PC
authentication pathway, and narrowing high-definition PC-reader and repair
markets. These developments support a fresh, format-specific inquiry without
claiming universal failure.

If the Office declines the preventive primary, petitioners request the same relief
during an objectively demonstrated last-reader window. The owner must still have
a functioning preservation-capable data reader; new compatible readers must no
longer be reasonably orderable and deliverable in the United States at a fair
price; and, after reasonable effort, the owner must be unable to obtain a
qualifying replacement or competent repair at a fair price.
Lack of a new, unused, materially equivalent authorized edition on comparable
durable terms at a fair price is an additional severable fallback, not a condition
of the primary request.
