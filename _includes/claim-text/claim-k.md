# CLAIM K — CLASS-WIDE RELIEF FOR MOTION PICTURES ON PROTECTED CONSUMER DISCS

## Item B. Description of Proposed New Exemption

This petition respectfully asks the Office for a class-wide remedy for motion pictures on protected consumer optical discs. It does not replace the narrower petitions filed separately. Those petitions show the same controls repeatedly blocking different lawful uses as technology changes. Instead of requiring another narrow exemption each time, petitioner asks the Office to protect users of this class for three years once the statutory showing is made.

Petitioner recognizes that, since 2006, the Office has refined classes by the particular user and use. It respectfully asks the Office to reconsider the more work-centered approach used in 2000, under which examples of harm helped identify the class without turning each example into a separate condition on relief. If the full request is denied, each narrower alternative can stand alone.

### (1) Types of copyrighted works that need to be accessed

Motion pictures, including television programs, videos, and supplementary motion-picture works, on lawfully made prerecorded consumer discs distributed to the public: CSS-protected DVDs; AACS-protected HD DVDs; standard Blu-ray Discs protected by AACS and any applicable BD+; and AACS2-protected Ultra HD Blu-ray Discs. Each format may be considered separately. UMD, games, software, streaming services, and downloads are excluded.

### (2) Physical media, devices, or services through which the works are accessed

People access these works through optical readers or players, including computer drives and consumer playback equipment. This request concerns only controls on the source discs, not servers, accounts, remotely supplied content, HDCP, or another output control.

### (3) Purposes for which the works need to be accessed

Lawful purposes include private disc-present playback without a fixed copy; private-home filtering under § 110(11); criticism or comment using short portions; disability access; public-domain-only use; qualifying replacement or preservation; and restoration of a specifically promised bundled copy.

These uses are examples of the recurring problem, not limits on the primary request. If the Office finds that the prohibition is adversely affecting users of this class, the primary request asks that it not apply to those users during the three-year period. Alternatively, relief may be limited to an independently noninfringing use. Infringement would remain unlawful.

### (4) Types of users who want access

The primary covers users of works in the class without limiting them by ownership or occupation. Narrower alternatives cover adversely affected users; consumers currently possessing or authorized to use a disc, including owners, renters, borrowers, library patrons, and household members; and owners who lawfully acquired and continuously possess the disc. Commercial status alone would not exclude a user, but circumvention services are not covered.

### (5) Barriers that currently exist or are likely to exist in the near future

CSS, AACS, BD+, and AACS2 can prevent access when no working licensed pathway is available. Authentication shutdowns, unsupported platforms, aging discs, and declining reader availability create present and likely barriers during 2027–2030. As conditions change, narrow activity-specific exemptions can leave new lawful uses uncovered.

In a qualifying case, the disc, reader, and other equipment would work, and circumvention would enable the lawful use. The user refrains because § 1201(a)(1)(A) prohibits it. That prohibition is the remaining legal barrier.

Each format, control, user group, purpose, and narrower condition may be considered separately. Nothing requested would excuse infringement, authorize circumvention services or tools, affect § 1201(a)(2) or § 1201(b), override a contract, permit unauthorized system access, or require anyone to provide content or operate a service.
