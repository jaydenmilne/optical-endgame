# CLAIM F — DISC-SOURCE BUNDLED DIGITAL-COPY RESTORATION

## 1. Types of copyrighted works

Motion pictures, including television programs, videos, and supplementary
motion-picture works.

## 2. Physical media, devices, and services

Lawfully made and lawfully acquired DVDs protected by CSS, standard Blu-ray Discs
protected by AACS and any applicable BD+, and Ultra HD Blu-ray Discs protected by
AACS2, in consumer packages expressly represented as including a title-specific
authorized digital copy.

## 3. Purpose of the requested access

To make one secured, owner-only, local, noncommercial substitute no broader in title,
version, audiovisual quality, content, functionality, users, or audience than the
represented digital copy. It would be destroyed upon sale, gift, disposal, or loss of
the package. This is not a general disc-copying request; distribution, transfer,
network availability, public or commercial use, circumvention services, and
trafficking are excluded.

## 4. Types of users

Individual owners must continuously possess the disc and package. One branch covers a
benefit validly redeemed before provider functionality ceased. The other covers a new
first-retail purchaser, or intended recipient, who possesses the original unused code,
promptly attempted redemption, and objectively shows publisher-set expiration.
Expiration may be shown by a provider response or documented expiration, failure, and
support inquiry yielding neither a cure nor a specific alternative reason after other
causes are reasonably excluded. No owner-held restoration route or materially
equivalent, no-charge replacement may remain available. The two histories are
severable.

## 5. Current or likely barriers

Funimation's website and services ended on April 2, 2024, and Crunchyroll states that
previously available Funimation digital copies are no longer supported or available.
Warner Bros. states that expiration runs from title release rather than purchase and
that expired codes are not renewed or replaced. The Funimation shutdown followed the
prior cycle's Round 1 comment deadline, making this loss newly concrete in this cycle.

The service or code failure creates the need for relief but is not the access control.
The complete motion picture remains on the disc, and technically feasible
circumvention would make the bounded substitute without obtaining server content.
Owners refrain because § 1201(a)(1)(A) prohibits bypassing the disc access control.
But for that prohibition, they could make the representation-bounded substitute
claimed to be noninfringing; the disc control is the remaining legal barrier after
licensed alternatives and non-TPM causes are excluded.
