# CLAIM C — DAMAGED OR DETERIORATING OPTICAL-DISC REPLACEMENT

## Item B. Description of Proposed New Exemption

### 1. Types of copyrighted works

Motion pictures, including television programs, videos, and supplementary motion-picture works, distributed on protected optical discs.

### 2. Physical media and devices

The works are stored on lawfully made and lawfully acquired discs owned by individuals: DVD-Video/CSS; HD DVD/AACS; standard Blu-ray Disc/AACS and any applicable BD+; and Ultra HD Blu-ray Disc/AACS2. The disc must remain sufficiently readable in a compatible optical drive. The one replacement could reside on secured, owner-controlled local or removable storage or in one cloud account. A cloud copy would be encrypted before upload with an owner-controlled key and could not be shared, publicly linked, or yield another retained usable copy or separate audience.

### 3. Purpose of access

The purpose is to make one secured, noncommercial personal replacement while an owned disc is actually damaged or objectively deteriorating but remains recoverable. An individualized, documented condition must materially impair or threaten reliable access under a recognized failure mechanism, or produce a reproducible read or playback error under an objective protocol. Age, format, or suspected-cohort membership alone would not qualify.

The replacement could contain no more motion-picture content than the disc. The original would be retained and retired from ordinary viewing once the replacement is used. Private, noncommercial viewing by the owner or household as one audience would be permitted. Distribution, lending, separate sale, public availability or performance, access by another person or household, commercial circumvention, and media-streaming services would be excluded. On voluntary transfer, the replacement would accompany the original or be destroyed; it could remain after documented unreadability or involuntary loss or destruction.

### 4. Users seeking access

The users are individual owners in continuous lawful possession of a qualifying disc that can be recovered before failure becomes total. Each format is severable. The primary class would not compel repurchase or make another version's availability dispositive. Exact-edition market-check, stronger-diagnostic, owner-only, and local-storage-only conditions remain available as fallbacks, without requiring a used-disc search.

### 5. Existing and likely barriers

Physical failure creates the deadline, but the applicable control—CSS, AACS and any BD+ employed, or AACS2—creates the legal barrier to making a usable replacement. The need is newly concrete this cycle: a 2025 public report reproduced a Warner Bros. statement concerning potential playback problems affecting select 2006–08 DVDs and limits on same-title replacements, while the 2024 renewal record documented continuing disc deterioration.

Qualified owners refrain because § 1201(a)(1) prohibits the circumvention needed to recover the protected content. But for that prohibition, they could make one bounded replacement before the source becomes unreadable; after total failure, an exemption would come too late. The class would not authorize infringement or trafficking in circumvention technology.
