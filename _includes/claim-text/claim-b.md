# CLAIM B — UNSUPPORTED-PLATFORM OPTICAL-DISC PLAYBACK

## UNSUPPORTED-PLATFORM AND ONE-DISPLAY PLAYBACK

### Item B. Description of Proposed New Exemption

#### 1. Types of copyrighted works that need to be accessed

Motion pictures, including television programs, videos, and supplementary motion-picture works, distributed on DVD or standard (non-UHD) Blu-ray Disc.

#### 2. Physical media or devices on which the works are stored or accessed

The works are stored on lawfully made and lawfully acquired DVDs protected by CSS and standard Blu-ray Discs protected by AACS and any applicable BD+. An individual owner would play the disc from a compatible optical drive on an owner-controlled computing device. The principal severable configurations are DVD and standard-Blu-ray playback on GNU/Linux and comparable identified open-source desktop systems, and standard-Blu-ray playback on Windows 11 on Arm. Other exact platforms qualify only when the source-disc access control is the remaining barrier and no licensed pathway supplies the claimed use.

As an independently severable branch, the computing device could provide live playback to one owner-controlled portable display within the same private premises by direct wire or an authenticated, encrypted private local network not routed over the public Internet. Ultra HD Blu-ray/AACS2 is addressed in a separate petition.

#### 3. Purposes for which the works need to be accessed

The purpose is contemporaneous, private, local, noncommercial playback from the disc. The primary activity is direct playback on the computing device. The severable one-display branch would allow that device to serve as the optical-disc host for one portable display and one private household audience within the same premises. The disc would remain present throughout playback, and only transitory playback and transport data would be used. No complete or substantially complete decrypted motion picture would remain reasonably reconstructable after the session or at either endpoint.

The request excludes making or retaining a movie file, later playback from a copy, recording, public-Internet routing, cloud relays, remote access, distribution, public performance, commercial circumvention services, rented or borrowed discs, multiple receivers, and a separate audience.

#### 4. Types of users who want access

The users are individual owners in continuous lawful possession of the disc who cannot complete the claimed use through a licensed, operational, reasonably available U.S. consumer pathway after reasonable configuration efforts. They control the computing device and, for the severable one-display branch, the single portable display. A pathway must be currently obtainable and usable for the claimed use on the devices employed; an obsolete listing, unavailable legacy product, or theoretical license is not sufficient. Same-room, direct-wired, and owner-only viewing are narrower alternatives for the one-display branch.

#### 5. Barriers that currently exist or are likely to exist in the near future

On a qualifying configuration, CSS, AACS, and any applicable BD+ prevent an otherwise functional device from decrypting and rendering the protected disc. Current evidence identifies gaps in the licensed-product market. Licensed Linux products existed historically, but Fluendo ended distribution of its Linux DVD player in 2021. CyberLink states that current PowerDVD versions do not support Blu-ray Disc playback on Qualcomm-based Windows 11 on Arm systems. Current consumer PC products also ship with, or officially support, Linux-based SteamOS, making this a present consumer-platform problem rather than merely a preference to replace a supported operating system.

Each qualifying configuration must have an otherwise functional device, operating-system version, optical drive, and playback path capable of rendering a comparable unencrypted authored disc, leaving CSS, AACS, or any applicable BD+—not an output control or ordinary incompatibility—as the remaining barrier. The one-display branch would apply only after the same live use has lacked a licensed, operational, reasonably available U.S. consumer pathway for at least six months and would cease if such a pathway becomes reasonably available. It is limited to an otherwise functional one-receiver pipeline enabled by circumvention of the source-disc control—not HDCP or another output or rights control—and leaving no reconstructable movie at either endpoint. But for 17 U.S.C. § 1201(a)(1), qualifying owners could bypass the source-disc barrier and engage in the bounded private playback described above.

Direct playback and the one-display branch are independently severable.
