# CLAIM H — DISC-PRESENT AUTOMATIC PRIVATE-HOME FILTERING

## 1. Types of copyrighted works to be accessed

Motion pictures, including television programs, videos, and supplementary
motion-picture works.

## 2. Physical media or devices on which the works are stored or accessed

The motion pictures are embodied on lawfully made and lawfully acquired DVDs
protected by CSS, standard Blu-ray Discs protected by AACS and any applicable
BD+, or Ultra HD Blu-ray Discs protected by AACS2. The owner accesses the work
locally from the particular disc using consumer optical-disc and playback
equipment in the home. DVD/CSS, standard Blu-ray/AACS and any applicable BD+,
and UHD/AACS2 are independently severable branches of this one petition.

## 3. Purposes for which the works need to be accessed

The owner seeks to apply automatic mute or skip instructions to limited
portions of audio or video content, by or at the direction of a member of the
owner's private household, during contemporaneous private-home playback
directly from the authorized disc. Congress expressly made this bounded
filtering use noninfringing in 17 U.S.C. § 110(11).

The disc will remain present throughout playback. Usable filter instructions
must already exist; they may be preexisting or household-authored. Any decrypted
motion-picture data will be transitory; no fixed altered version or complete or
substantially complete decrypted movie file will be created or retained. The
system may mute or skip but will not add replacement audio or video content.
Distribution, public performance, a separate audience, transmission outside the
private household, and remote filtering services are excluded.

## 4. Types of users who want access

The users are individual owners who remain in continuous lawful possession of
the particular discs. A member of the owner's private household selects or
directs the filtering during private-home viewing.

## 5. Current or likely barriers preventing access

CSS, AACS and any applicable BD+, and AACS2 prevent otherwise functional local
filtering software from reading and rendering the authorized discs. The owner
must already have usable mute or skip instructions, and all other components of
the local filtering process must work. But for the prohibition in 17 U.S.C.
§ 1201(a)(1), the owner would circumvent the source-disc access control and
complete the noninfringing § 110(11) performance.

The licensed physical-disc filtering market has materially contracted.
ClearPlay now says that it no longer accepts DVD or Blu-ray filter requests,
has substantially reduced physical-media filter production, and has focused on
streaming since 2021, while still claiming a legacy pathway for some U.S. DVDs
and standard Blu-ray Discs. The proposed exemption applies to a particular disc
edition only when, after reasonable configuration, inquiry, and testing, no
licensed, operational, reasonably available U.S. consumer pathway or reliable
noncircumventing local-control method can perform the same selected automatic
filtering. Missing remote metadata, ordinary hardware or software failure,
region incompatibility, and a measure functioning only as an output or right
control do not qualify.

The request is limited to the owner's act of access-control circumvention. It
does not authorize trafficking under 17 U.S.C. § 1201(a)(2) or (b).
