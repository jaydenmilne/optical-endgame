# CLAIM J — AACS2-PROTECTED UHD EXCERPTS FOR INDIVIDUAL CRITICISM OR COMMENT

## Item B. Description of Proposed New Exemption

Petitioner seeks to permit an individual owner of an AACS2-protected Ultra HD Blu-ray Disc to circumvent AACS2 solely to obtain a short, source-faithful excerpt for noninfringing criticism or comment.

### 1. Types of copyrighted works that need to be accessed

Motion pictures, including television programs, videos, and supplementary motion-picture works, on lawfully made and lawfully acquired Ultra HD Blu-ray Discs protected by AACS2. Only the short portion reasonably necessary for the critical purpose would be used.

### 2. Physical media, devices, or services through which the works are accessed

The works are stored on Ultra HD Blu-ray Discs and accessed through a compatible optical reader and the individual's editing workflow. The request concerns AACS2 on the source disc—not an output control, unavailable remote content, or a service credential.

### 3. Purposes for which the works need to be accessed

The individual would incorporate the short portion into a video, film, or nonfiction multimedia e-book for criticism or comment. The excerpt use must be fair use under 17 U.S.C. § 107 and is not disqualified merely because the new work is monetized, commissioned, licensed, sold, distributed for profit, or otherwise commercial. The request excludes substitutive uses and dissemination of a decrypted source. Any complete or substantially complete decrypted technical intermediate must be independently noninfringing, access-restricted, and promptly destroyed.

Severable narrower alternatives are the current noncommercial-video, documentary or specified-other-film, and nonfiction-multimedia-e-book categories, extended only to AACS2-protected Ultra HD Blu-ray Discs.

### 4. Types of users who want access

Individuals who own the disc, remain in continuous lawful possession, and create the critical work alone, with collaborators, or for an entity. The request does not cover a commercial circumvention service or trafficking prohibited by 17 U.S.C. § 1201(a)(2) or § 1201(b).

### 5. Barriers that currently exist or are likely to exist in the near future

Current exemptions cover narrower critical uses from CSS-protected DVDs, AACS-protected standard Blu-ray Discs, and protected digital transmissions, but not AACS2-protected Ultra HD Blu-ray Discs. This request is newly relevant because some UHD editions contain distinct cuts, restorations, or presentation attributes, while licensed Windows-PC UHD-disc playback has ceased, narrowing potential source workflows.

The class would apply when the individual reasonably believes no reasonably available noncircumventing source or method can provide the portion, version, or audiovisual quality reasonably necessary for the critical purpose. The disc, reader, and remaining workflow must be functional; AACS2—not missing content, an output control, hardware failure, or ordinary incompatibility—must be the remaining barrier. But for § 1201(a)(1)'s prohibition, the individual would circumvent AACS2 and make the source-faithful short excerpt, but refrains because the prohibition applies even where the excerpt use would be fair.
