# Claims we've filed

These are the claims that we filed on August 24th, 2026. We need to find 
compelling testimonials for all of these.

- [**A - UHD Blu-ray Playback on PC**]({{ '/claims/4k-playback/' | relative_url }}): They 
  took 4K PC playback away from us, and that's not fair.
- [**B - Playback on unlicensed playforms**]({{ '/claims/modern-platform-playback/' | relative_url }}):
  The computers that most people use are capable of playing discs but can't for 
  legal reasons. Let's fix that.
- [**C - Failing discs**]({{ '/claims/damaged-discs/' | relative_url }}): There are well 
  documented cases of perfectly stored discs failing. Owners should be able to
  make copies before its too late.
- [**D - Drive End of Production**]({{ '/claims/dead-drives/' | relative_url }}): Manufacturing
  of Blu-ray drives has largely ceased as of 2026, and existing drives are aging.
  It's time to make back up copies while they still work.
- [**F - Bundled codes don't work or stop working**]({{ '/claims/bundled-codes/' | relative_url }}): Studios aren't honoring the digital codes they include with discs, or revoking codes that did. If they do, we should be able to make our own digital copy.
- [**H - Filtering discs**]({{ '/claims/filtering-media/' | relative_url }}): Congress passed 
the [Family Entertainment and Copyright Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_Entertainment_and_Copyright_Act) 20 years ago so we could filter movies. DRM prevents us from doing so.
- [**I - Public domain discs**]({{ '/claims/public-domain-works/' | relative_url }}): Works are 
entering the public domain again, and we should be able to extract public domain
content from discs protected by DRM.
- [**J - 4K Fair Use**]({{ '/claims/4k-fair-use/' | relative_url }}): There is no way to extract bits of copyrighted content from a 4K UHD Blu-ray
  for fair use purposes.  
- [**K - These Exemptions Are Too Narrow**]({{ '/claims/k/' | relative_url }}): In 2006 the Library of Congress for its convenience it should
  only give exemptions to specific _uses_ of DRM circumvention (the above), not particular _classes of work_ - like all DVDs or Blu-rays.
  That's too narrow and ticky tacky, and has lead to 20 years of bickering. 
  
  Congress allowed class wide relief for entire categories of work (like all DVDs) - the Library of Congress's
  interpretation is too narrow.
